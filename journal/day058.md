## 🎯 Executive Summary
Applied practical Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) and advanced search techniques in a real-world defensive situation. The investigation successfully uncovered a phishing infrastructure targeting a peer. By pivoting from a single image lure and utilizing advanced search operators, the attack vector was neutralized, preventing credentials exposure while maintaining strict adherence to legal and privacy boundaries.

---

## 🔍 Investigative Methodology & Technical Execution

### 1. Visual Intelligence via Yandex Images
The attacker utilized a highly convincing, custom-branded image lure embedded within the email body to bypass standard text-based spam filters.
* **Action:** Extracted the raw image file from the email and performed a reverse image lookup via Yandex Images.
* **Findings:** Yandex's facial recognition and OCR capabilities successfully mapped the image to an unindexed corporate template altered by a malicious actor.
* **Pivot:** Identified distinct typographic anomalies and artifacts unique to a known threat actor group deploying localized phishing kits.

### 2. Advanced Exploration via Google Dorking
To uncover hidden infrastructure related to the phishing lure, specific Google Dorking parameters were deployed against the suspicious domains harvested from the email headers.
* `site:malicious-domain.com` 
  * *Result:* Revealed indexed directories containing active login harvesting pages.
* `filetype:php site:malicious-domain.com`
  * *Result:* Isolated the backend scripts responsible for processing stolen credentials.
* `intitle:"index of" "lure-assets"`
  * *Result:* Exposed an open directory on a compromised staging server hosting identical visual assets used in the email.

### 3. Infrastructure & Vector Analysis
* **Header Analysis:** Inspected the raw email headers (`DKIM`, `SPF`, and `DMARC`). Discovered a soft-fail configuration (`spf=softfail`), indicating sender spoofing.
* **Domain Age Verification:** Ran a `WHOIS` lookup on the hosting domain. The domain was registered less than 48 hours prior through an anonymized registrar, a definitive indicator of malicious intent.

---

## 🛡️ Defensive Actions & Mitigation
1. **Immediate Triage:** Advised the target to isolate the email, avoid interacting with hyperlinked elements, and refrain from downloading remote assets.
2. **Infrastructure Blocklisting:** Added the malicious domain and associated sender IP addresses to the local gateway blocklist.
3. **IOC Documentation:** Logged the Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)—including file hashes of the image lure and the malicious URLs—for security information and event management (SIEM) correlation.
4. **Awareness Briefing:** Conducted an impromptu phishing awareness walkthrough, demonstrating how to inspect hyperlinks without clicking them and highlighting the visual inconsistencies found during the OSINT phase.

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways & Lessons Learned
* **Visual Traps:** Attackers increasingly rely on image-only payloads to defeat text-based heuristic analysis. Reverse image engines are vital tools for defensive analysts.
* **Dorking Agility:** Google Dorking remains an incredibly fast, passive method to map out attacker infrastructure without directly interacting with malicious servers or triggering alarms.
* **Privacy Compliance:** Passive OSINT tools allow defenders to gather exhaustive intelligence on external threats without crossing ethical boundaries or violating privacy standards.
